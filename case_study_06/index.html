01 — Education

Personal Art & Design Portfolio
Consulting

"For about ten years, I ran a small studio that helped students build their portfolios for art and design schools — mainly the top seven in the US, Canada, and the UK. Every year, around five to six students were actively working with me, and twenty to thirty people came in for consultations. It wasn't just teaching. I managed schedules, stayed in daily contact with parents, handled all the paperwork for international applications, and built relationships with schools abroad. About ten teachers passed through over the years — including graduate students from the University of Toronto and working designers from the UK and the US."

"When COVID hit, the number of students gradually declined. In June 2026, I closed the chapter after ten years. It wasn't easy to let go of something I had built from scratch. But I think everything I learned — about communication, about patience, about designing systems for people — that stays with me."

RDA in the Classroom — Teaching, Mentoring & Community

About This Video

“This is not a great piece of content, and it’s certainly not a reel. It’s simply a recording of an ordinary session — a teacher passing information to students in the most straightforward way possible. Senior practitioners and teachers need to lower themselves in order to stay flexible. That posture gives learners the time and space to grow, to push past their limits, and to find the courage to attempt the next hurdle.”

“Through my students, I learned how to become the background: sometimes the hurdle itself, sometimes the starting gun, sometimes the referee calling a fault, and sometimes the roar of the crowd. That kind of experience is not easily come by — and I know it. In real professional work, if the client is the protagonist, I need to be able to become the background. And for that to happen, the client must also come prepared with what they truly need — because no amount of objective language, however rich or precise, can be persuasive without the creative experience of the person delivering it. Without that subjective depth, even the most articulate argument fails to land beautifully.”

“This is not merely philosophical. If you need someone who can see both sides of what exists — or of what remains unseen — and who can go further still, to see the concrete details that form each side and the background that surrounds the whole: you may be satisfying.”

Education — Key Contributions

Curriculum & Materials

Developed and continuously refined the full teaching curriculum and all instructional materials. Was an early adopter of Notion when it first launched — using it as a student management hub and shared resource platform before it became mainstream.

Admissions & Mentoring

Guided students to successful admissions at top art and design schools across Canada, the US, and the UK. Provided ongoing mentoring and built lasting professional connections that extended well beyond graduation.

Economic Impact

Generated approximately 100,000 CAD in annual tuition revenue and 33,000 CAD in additional income from founding through 2020. Created employment for 5+ design professionals in the local market.

Community

Built a close-knit studio community where students, teachers, and alumni stayed connected. The network continues to this day — a quiet proof that the work was about more than portfolios.

Following the Guidelines — Sources, References & Inspirations

Curriculum Philosophy

“At RDA, we follow the official Toronto graphic design guidelines — and we also steal, carefully, from the best sources we can find. We always credit what we borrow. The core philosophy and teaching principles are fixed; but the methods, the tools, and the way we teach must keep pace with the times — and with the technology each era produces.”

“In the age of AI, the moment you stop and declare yourself done, you risk being left far behind. And yet, you cannot afford to never pause — to look back, to sit with an idea, to go deep. Both are necessary. That tension is the work.”

“That’s why I love what Picasso said: “Good artists borrow; great artists steal.” It’s not about taking without thought — it’s about absorbing something so fully that it becomes yours, transformed.”

RDA Mobile & Desktop App
Sketch → Adobe XD → Mockup

"I don't think of what I did as a side job. For me, RDA was a way of practising creative confidence — the same thing I was trying to teach. I wanted to build something that was entirely mine, from the brand to the tools we used. So I designed an app for the studio: starting from hand-drawn sketches, moving into Adobe XD, and finishing with a working mockup for both mobile and desktop."

"RDA is done now, but the thinking behind it isn't. The ten years of quietly holding it together, keeping it going — that was also a test of my own endurance. Everything in this studio carried my values. It wasn't just design work. It was a way of showing who I am."

Student Collaboration & Real-World Practice

“These projects were built together with students — from ideation through to final delivery. Clients were satisfied. Revenue was real. And every student who participated experienced the full A-to-Z of a live brief: the conversations, the revisions, the decisions that aren’t in any textbook.”

“None of it was easy. Bringing students into real client work means carrying more responsibility, not less — you have to protect them while also holding them to a real standard. But what I found, every time, was that the teachers learned more than anyone. That’s not a lesson you can plan for. It just happens, when you’re honest about what you don’t know.”

04 — Art Practice

Making by Hand,
Thinking by Eye

"I like making things with my hands. I also like moving ideas onto a screen, and lately I've been spending time with AI as a kind of new communication. But beyond all of that, there's something I keep coming back to — shining a light on problems that don't seem like problems yet. That, to me, is what art does."

"I don't think design can solve problems it hasn't first learned to see. Art is how I train that seeing. When I asked to ride the kindergarten bus for two months before designing the app — that came from this. From the habit of looking before drawing. Sometimes that makes me a troublemaker. I turn things that weren't problems into problems, and problems into solutions. The means become the end, and the end becomes the means. That's not always comfortable. But it's honest."

"These works aren't here to prove anything. They're here because they're part of the same practice — the same hand, the same eye, the same person."
