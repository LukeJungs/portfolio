Context

UKIN was a food and beverage startup incubator that grew from seed funding to Series B — a company that built brands from the ground up. As a founding design member, the challenge was not to refine an existing identity, but to create one from nothing. The flagship project was AZOTO, a liquid nitrogen ice cream concept. There was no name, no logo, no packaging, and no physical space.

The concept was scientifically precise: ice cream made fresh at -196°C using liquid nitrogen, in full view of the customer. The brand needed to match that precision — sharp, technical, and theatrical — while remaining accessible enough to draw queues on the streets of Hongdae.

The brand had to be as dramatic as the product itself — a performance of science and craft, visible from the street.

Scope

  • 01 Logo & Trademark Custom geometric mark, Korean/English wordmark, trademark registration (제 45-2014-0004374)
  • 02 Brand System Colour palette, typography, brand language and tone across all touchpoints
  • 03 Packaging Paper cups (BR150cc), tissue paper, takeaway packaging design
  • 04 Environmental Design Store facade, illuminated 3D signage, interior wall graphics, menu boards
  • 05 Multi-location Rollout Brand consistency across Hongdae, Anyang, and additional franchise locations

Brand Identity

The logo was built from a single geometric insight: the ice cream cone. Starting with the most fundamental form — a circle resting on a triangle — the design evolved through three stages. First, the cone shape was abstracted into a letterform. Second, the triangle and inverted triangle were combined into a structural, architectural mark. Third, the outline was refined to create a sharp, non-decorative shape that would read clearly at any scale, from a paper cup to an illuminated 3D facade sign.

The result was a custom typographic mark that functions simultaneously as an abstract symbol and a legible wordmark. The geometric precision of the mark — registered as trademark 제 45-2014-0004374 — communicated the scientific rigour of the liquid nitrogen process while maintaining the visual drama needed to stop foot traffic on a busy Seoul street.

Space & Packaging

The identity was not confined to paper. Every physical touchpoint — from the BR150cc paper cup to the architectural facade — was treated as an extension of the brand. The store exterior used the geometric language of the logo as a structural element: the facade itself became a large-scale interpretation of the mark, with LED-trimmed edges that echoed the logo's sharp outline.

Inside, the brand maintained its high-contrast aesthetic. The illuminated 3D logo sign became the centrepiece of the interior, creating a dramatic backdrop for the liquid nitrogen ice cream making process. The atmospheric lighting — deep blues and sharp whites — reinforced the scientific, theatrical quality of the brand.

Outcome

AZOTO launched and expanded to multiple franchise locations across Seoul, including Hongdae and Anyang. The brand's visual identity — sharp, scientific, and theatrical — proved immediately effective. Queues formed outside the Hongdae location from opening day, and the brand was featured on Olive TV's Tasty Road, one of Korea's most-watched food and lifestyle programs.

The project demonstrated that a cohesive brand identity, built with rigour from a single geometric concept, can drive tangible business outcomes. The trademark, the packaging, the spatial design — each element reinforced the others, creating a total brand experience that was unmistakable at every scale.

Side Projects within UKIN In-House Design

Creative Work Beyond the Brief

Within the intensity of a startup environment, maintaining creative balance is not a luxury — it is a discipline. These two side projects, both developed within UKIN's in-house design team, represent the space where risk was free and experimentation was the only rule. The energy cultivated here always found its way back into the main work.

Side Project 01

Non-Profit · 2015

Pure6 — pH of Creative Confidence

A non-profit skincare concept centred around pH6 — the optimal level of healthy skin. Scientifically precise, clean, and purposeful. The project provided a space for free artwork exploration: posters, typographic experiments, visual systems — without the constraints of a client brief.

It was a deliberate act of creative confidence building. The philosophy: maintaining a balance between input and output inevitably leads to the next step.

Every side project is an investment in the next main project. Pure6 was where risk was free — and that freedom always found its way back into the work.

Though Pure6 no longer exists independently — it was eventually absorbed into a larger, now well-known brand — the experimental energy it fostered directly influenced the rigour and confidence brought to flagship projects like AZOTO.

Side Project 02

Artwork Collaboration · 2015

Sammontana Korea — Art Meets Brand

Sammontana is one of Italy's most iconic ice cream brands. The Korea launch, managed through UKIN, presented an opportunity to bridge two distinct visual cultures — Italian heritage and Korean contemporary design sensibility.

The project involved artwork collaboration with independent artists, creating a series of limited-edition visual identities and packaging concepts that brought Sammontana's playful, artisanal spirit into a Korean market context.

Collaboration is a design tool. Working with artists outside the brief expanded what the brand could be — and what I could do as a designer.

The Sammontana Korea project reinforced a core belief: the best brand work happens at the intersection of rigorous craft and genuine creative exchange. Every collaboration since has carried that lesson forward.
