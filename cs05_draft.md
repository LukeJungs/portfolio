# CS05: Between Idea and Object
**Subtitle:** Art Thinking, Design Making

## Meta Strip
- **Role:** Fine Artist · Graphic Designer · Art Director
- **Scope:** Fine Art · Conceptual Sketching · Industrial Translation · Spatial Design
- **Focus:** The intersection of artistic ideation and commercial execution.

## 1. Context (Origin)
**Images:** `3D_Ceramic_sketch.jpeg` (Hero), `artwork_1.png`, `artwork_2.png`, `artwork_3.png` (Grid)
**Text Draft:**
My foundation is in fine arts. Before I designed packaging systems or digital storefronts, I studied form, volume, and material tension. The ceramic sketches and mixed-media artworks here are not separate from my design practice — they are its engine. 

Fine art taught me how to think without constraints, to explore spatial relationships, and to trust the iterative process of making. Design, on the other hand, taught me how to channel that exploration into functional, scalable, and compliant systems. This portfolio is the ongoing dialogue between those two disciplines.

**Pull Quote:**
"Art is the freedom to ask the question. Design is the discipline to answer it at scale."

## 2. Translation (Ideation)
**Images:** `V-TAG_sketches.webp`, `V-TAG_sketches2.jpeg` (Side-by-side)
**Text Draft:**
The transition from an abstract concept to a commercial product begins on paper. In the V-TAG project, the initial industrial design and packaging architecture were not born in CAD or Illustrator, but through rapid hand-drawn iterations. 

Sketching allows for an immediacy that software often interrupts. It is where the artistic intuition for proportion meets the rigid realities of manufacturing constraints, battery placement, and Health Canada's regulatory typography requirements.

## 3. Iteration (Prototyping)
**Images:** `V-TAG_package_목업_1.webp`, `V-TAG_package_목업_2.webp`, `V-TAG_package_목업_3.webp` (3-col grid)
**Text Draft:**
A sketch is a promise; a prototype is a proof. The translation from 2D drawings to physical packaging mockups requires a shift in mindset. Here, the artist's concern for aesthetics must align with the designer's responsibility for production literacy. 

These mockups represent the crucial phase where material thickness, fold tolerances, and print finishes are tested. It is the phase where ideas are subjected to the friction of reality.

## 4. Production (Execution)
**Images:** `designprocess.webp`, `Screenshot2026-07-08at7.25.25PM.webp`, `photoshooting.webp`
**Text Draft:**
Execution is where the dual identity of artist and designer becomes a distinct advantage. Understanding the physical nature of an object informs how it should be digitally rendered, photographed, and presented.

Whether directing a product photoshoot or managing the meticulous alignment of bilingual compliance text on a digital dieline, the goal remains consistent: preserving the integrity of the original idea while satisfying every commercial and regulatory demand.

## 5. Activation (Spatial)
**Images:** `V-TAG_부스1.png`, `V-TAG_부스2.png`, `V-TAG_부스3.png` (Full bleed + 2-col)
**Text Draft:**
A brand is ultimately a spatial experience. Designing the V-TAG exhibition booth was an exercise in scaling a 10cm package into a 10-meter architectural environment. 

The fine art background resurfaces here — thinking about human circulation, negative space, and monumental scale. The booth is not just a display; it is the physical embodiment of the brand identity, built to facilitate B2B interactions while maintaining the stark, disciplined aesthetic of the core design system.

## 6. Outcome (Reflection)
**Text Draft:**
Operating between art and design is not about blurring the lines, but knowing exactly where they are. 

The artistic process provides the conceptual depth and formal sensitivity needed to create work that resonates. The design process provides the rigor, the compliance, and the systems-thinking required to deploy that work across international markets. Embracing both allows me to navigate the space between the ideal and the possible — turning raw ideas into tangible, functional objects.
