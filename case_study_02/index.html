Context

Urban Dive Marketing positioned itself as a business accelerator and digital marketing agency at the heart of downtown Toronto — steps from Dundas Square in downtown Toronto. The company served a wide range of clients across Canada, with an international presence through design teams in Pakistan and China. The ambition was clear: help businesses grow through strategy, digital presence, and brand awareness.

Yet the internal visual language did not yet match that ambition. The challenge was not simply to redesign a logo or produce a brochure. It was to establish a comprehensive brand ecosystem — one that could be understood, applied, and maintained by teams across continents, interns in Toronto, and collaborators in Seoul.

"Business is a human activity and not control+c control+v."

This philosophy, printed on the cover of the 2019 UDM booklet, became the guiding principle for all design decisions. Every touchpoint — from the logo grid to the infographic typography — had to feel considered, not templated.

Scope & Leadership

As Lead Graphic Designer, the role extended far beyond individual deliverables. The mandate covered the full spectrum of brand expression: logo and identity system, brand colour and tone, document typography standards, exhibition banners, booth design and production oversight, and collaboration with real estate agents and artists. Alongside the creative work, a significant portion of the role was operational — training interns in the Toronto office and guiding a team of approximately 20 designers near the capital of Pakistan to ensure global brand consistency.

Brand Identity System

The UDM identity was built on a modular logic. The primary mark — "Urban Dive" in bold weight with the distinctive UP arrow symbol — communicated upward momentum and digital fluency. The symbol was designed to function independently as a brand icon across digital and physical contexts, from app icons to exhibition signage.

A strict grid system governed all applications, ensuring that the logo retained its proportional integrity whether printed at business-card scale or blown up to a 3-metre exhibition banner. Sub-brands, including Urban Dive School, were developed within the same visual language — differentiated by colour while maintaining structural consistency.

The colour palette centred on a deep navy and a high-visibility yellow, a pairing that projected authority and energy simultaneously. These two values — credibility and dynamism — were the core of UDM's brand positioning.

Brand System

Every brand system is only as strong as its most repeated touchpoint — and for UDM, that was the business card. Designed in two versions (navy front / white back and white front / white back), the cards were produced with precise crop marks and inspected through the full printing process. The Heesun Jung card was among the first produced, serving as the quality benchmark for all subsequent print runs.

The logo system was extended to cover Urban Dive School as a sub-brand, maintaining structural consistency with the primary mark while differentiating through colour and weight. Both light and dark background versions were prepared, ensuring flexibility across digital and print applications — from email signatures to exhibition banners.

Underlying every deliverable was the discipline of the RGD (Registered Graphic Designers of Canada) Client/Design Brief — a two-page framework covering project objectives, target audience, key message, tone & manner, and project mandates. As an OCAD University graduate, applying this professional standard to every project was not a formality but a foundation: it ensured that design decisions were always traceable back to a strategic rationale, and that interns and international team members could understand the intent behind each creative choice.

Marketing Strategy

Urban Dive Marketing's value proposition was built on a clear-eyed understanding of the digital landscape: that design without strategy is noise, and strategy without design is invisible. The 2019 booklet opened with a direct challenge to clients — why go digital? — and answered it with data. At a time when 94% of negative business experiences were attributed to poor website UX, and 63% of consumers refused to engage with a business that lacked an online presence, the case for a coherent digital brand was not a recommendation. It was a business imperative.

The CPM (cost per thousand impressions) comparison — from $32 for newspaper down to $0.25 for Facebook Ads — served as the booklet's most persuasive spread, translating abstract marketing theory into a concrete financial argument. This was the kind of content that converted a sceptical CFO, not just a marketing manager. Designing this spread required understanding the audience's decision-making logic, not just their aesthetic preferences.

The design challenge was to make these arguments feel as authoritative as a strategy deck while remaining as readable as a magazine. High-contrast typography, a disciplined navy-and-yellow palette, and a consistent grid system ensured that the data communicated urgency without sacrificing credibility.

Editorial Design

The 2019 UDM Booklet served as both a client-facing manifesto and an internal benchmark for typographic standards. Its purpose was to communicate UDM's marketing funnel methodology — Research & Consulting, Strategy & Optimization, Execute & Analyze — in a format that felt as credible as the strategy itself.

The design used a high-contrast palette of deep navy and bright yellow, with custom infographics translating complex digital statistics into clear, scannable visual data. Every spread was governed by a consistent grid, ensuring that the booklet read as a unified document rather than a collection of pages.

The intern collaboration spread — designed by intern Karina Wu under direct mentorship — demonstrated the effectiveness of the training model: a junior designer producing work that met the same standard as the lead designer's output.

Selected Client Work

Beyond building UDM's internal brand, the role extended to client-facing design work. Abundance Real Estate Group — a Toronto-based brokerage — commissioned a full brand identity and print collateral package. The brief called for a premium, understated identity that would position the agency above the visual noise typical of the real estate sector.

The solution centred on a restrained typographic system: a serif wordmark in spaced capitals, a strictly black-and-white palette, and a card design where the back carried a single, confident line of copy — "I'd love to help you do the same." — rather than a list of services. The result was a business card that felt more like a personal letter than a marketing tool, reflecting the broker's relationship-first approach.

The full package — business cards (dark and light versions), a branded folder, and print production oversight — was delivered from brief to final print inspection, following the same RGD design brief process applied to all UDM client engagements.

Outcome

By centralising the design vision in Toronto and establishing clear guidelines for international teams, UDM transformed its visual presence from a fragmented collection of assets into a coherent brand ecosystem. The resulting identity system communicated the agency's authority as a digital accelerator, while the editorial booklet gave the sales team a credible, high-quality tool for client acquisition.

The mentorship structure — training interns in Toronto and guiding the Pakistan team remotely — ensured that every output, from a simple document template to a large-scale exhibition booth, adhered to the same standard of excellence. The result was a brand ecosystem that communicated UDM's authority as a digital accelerator, giving the sales team a credible, high-quality tool for client acquisition across Canada and internationally.

Every company needs someone to help them harness the potential of emerging technologies, new business models, and ever-changing human behaviours to craft the right strategy.
